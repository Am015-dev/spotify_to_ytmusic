Research date: 2026-10-13. Web only, 13 searches/fetches. Every concrete claim has a URL.
Anything not backed by a source is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Design proposals (section 4) are ours, not 2K's.

## 0. Headline finding: LEGO 2K Drive has NO track builder

- Not one source found describes a track editor, "Track Builder" or custom-race-track mode in LEGO 2K Drive.
  The game's Create/build mode is a **vehicle** editor (cars, off-roaders, boats).
  - Vehicle editor only: "create street cars, road cars and boats" (https://playday.one/2023/05/19/review-lego-2k-drive/),
    "an incredibly robust vehicle editor" (same review, as summarised by the search).
  - Garages are fast-travel points and "a starting point to build your own LEGO car in the powerful vehicle editor";
    the game has "26 tracks", all available "from the start" (https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/reviews/lego-2k-drive-review-some-missing-pieces/z0637).
  - Before launch, Game Rant wrote it "remains to be seen" whether a custom track builder would come, comparing it with Hot Wheels Unleashed
    (https://gamerant.com/lego-2k-drive-hot-wheels-unleashed-track-builder-customization-vehicle-good/).
  - Wikipedia lists vehicles from about 1,000 unique LEGO pieces and does not mention a track editor (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_2K_Drive).
  - A forum poster compared it with Hot Wheels Unleashed's track editor, implying fans expected one
    (https://www.sonicstadium.org/forums/topic/31522-lego-2k-drive-open-world-brick-based-racing-ps45-xbox-oneseries-switch-pc-releases-may-19th/).
- Status: the game was delisted on 2026-05-19, and online services shut down on 2027-05-31 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_2K_Drive).

## 1. What the 2K tracks and world DO have (the reference to match)

### Vehicle types and swapping
- Three forms: street car, off-roader, boat. The swap is **automatic by terrain**:
  "your vehicle automatically switches between a street car, off-roader or boat depending on the terrain"
  (https://racinggames.gg/article/lego-2k-drive-beginners-guide-5-essential-tips-and-tricks-you-need-to-know).
- It "will actually morph from street car to off-roader to speedboat automatically"
  (https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/reviews/lego-2k-drive-review-some-missing-pieces/z0637).
- It "auto-swaps between either your car, boat, or off-road ride of choice" (Kotaku, as quoted at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_2K_Drive).
  The land vehicle becomes a boat on entering water (same page).
- Surface types are therefore effectively: road (asphalt) → street car, off-road (dirt/grass) → off-roader, water → boat.
- TechRadar describes a "manual transformation" adding depth (a timed jump plus a switch to off-road on a sharp bend)
  (https://techradar.com/reviews/lego-2k-drive-review-one-of-the-best-racers-ever-built, from the search snippet;
  the full page did not load). **UNVERIFIED** how manual swapping works in detail. Treat auto-swap as the baseline.

### Driving verbs that tracks are built around
- Jump at will. A jump "just before the end of a ramp" gives "a handy speed boost"
  (https://racinggames.gg/article/lego-2k-drive-beginners-guide-5-essential-tips-and-tricks-you-need-to-know).
- Drifting "charges up your boost meter". Boost fills over time, but "smashing into destructible objects and pulling off drifts
  will charge it up faster" (same URL).
- Quick turns exist but "slow you down" (racinggames.gg URL above, as summarised by the search).
- Jumps help find "shortcuts and collectables" (racinggames.gg URL above).

### What reviewers said about the tracks
- "shortcuts are plentiful"; jump and boost at will "can lead to some tricky maneuvers"
  (https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/reviews/lego-2k-drive-review-some-missing-pieces/z0637).
- Tracks "showcase the fun of the LEGO world including its destructive elements"; crashing into things fills boost (same URL).
- IGN praised courses for "technical segments, environmental hazards, and rewarding shortcuts" (as quoted at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_2K_Drive).
- Courses go "across the skies and into nearby riverbeds" (Shacknews preview,
  https://www.shacknews.com/article/134720/lego-2k-drive-hands-on-preview-pc?amphtml=1, from the search summary).
- Criticisms: heavy rubber-banding gives most races the same progression, the spider-web trap forces jump-mashing,
  and the worlds feel small (https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/reviews/lego-2k-drive-review-some-missing-pieces/z0637).
  Lesson: hazards that take control away are disliked.
- Track variety is limited compared with Forza Horizon 5, but each track is visually distinct
  (https://playday.one/2023/05/19/review-lego-2k-drive/).

### Numbers I could NOT find for 2K tracks (all UNVERIFIED)

  and surface swaps per lap. No source gave these. Measuring them would need a gameplay video and a stopwatch.
  That is out of scope for a web-only pass.

## 2. Grid and snap rules: NON-2K references

### Hot Wheels Unleashed 2 track builder (NON-2K; the closest genre analogue)
Source: https://progameguides.com/hot-wheels-unleashed-2/track-builder-guide-hot-wheels-unleashed-2-turbocharged/
(the page returned 403 on fetch, so these facts come from the search summary of it):

## 3. What makes custom / arcade tracks fun (NON-2K design sources)

### Track width (Luke McMillan, "A Rational Approach To Racing Game Track Design", Gamasutra/Game Developer, 2011-09-06)
https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/feature-the-fundamentals-of-arcade-style-racing-track-design
- "each lane should be 1.6 car widths wide"
- "the road shoulder should be around 50 percent to 75 percent the width of a car"
- "making the road wider will make corners easier"; wider corners give "more margin for error".
- Corner difficulty depends on the distance between clipping points and how much correction the car needs.
  Compound corners have several clipping points.

### Layout and pacing (Magnopus, Fortnite race-track case study)
https://www.magnopus.com/blog/the-art-of-designing-a-memorable-race-track
- Vary turns: sharp and banked turns force braking, drifting or repositioning.
- Keep turns ≤ 90°: "any turn over 90 degrees led to collision issues". Split sharper ones into segments.
- **Splits**: "splits give them the opportunity to decide – left or right?" Each branch should feel different
  (sharper, shorter or safer). Test merge points for gaps.
- **Escalate**: "gradually increase in intensity and complexity".
- **Space out key moments** so each one lands. Use landmarks and effects "selectively".
- Put a ramp at a climax moment as a payoff.

### Shortcuts (Mario Kart, NON-2K; player opinion, not designer statements)
- Later Mario Kart games moved from a few big shortcuts to "a lot of little shortcuts" that save a few seconds
  (https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/feature/19000).
- Some MK8 shortcuts need a boost item; others are open to everyone
  (https://tkcreatedesign.myportfolio.com/mario-kart-mobile-application-design).
- Simple beginner circuits have the fewest hazards. Walls are used to stop corner-cutting
  (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Kart/Mario_Circuit).

## 4. Design targets for our builder (proposals; tune them in tPlay)

Rationale in brackets. "P" = our proposal, not a sourced fact.

### Track-level targets (checked by an automatic validator before a track can be raced)
1. **Lap time 45–90 s** at an average driver's pace (P; short laps suit a phone session. 2K and MK lap times are UNVERIFIED).
2. **Elevation range ≥ 6 m per track** (P; 2K courses go "across the skies and into riverbeds" [Shacknews]). At least one climb or drop of ≥ 3 m.
3. **≥ 2 jumps per lap with ≥ 1.0 s of air** each, spaced ≥ 8 s apart ("space out key moments" [Magnopus]; the jump verb is core [racinggames.gg]).
4. **≥ 2 routes**: at least one split where each branch is different (shorter but riskier, or longer but safer) [Magnopus].
   Many small shortcuts that save 0.5–2 s each, not one route that skips the lap [NWR thread].
5. **≥ 1 land↔water swap and ≥ 1 road↔off-road swap per lap**, with automatic vehicle swap on surface change [racinggames.gg, Gamepressure].
6. **Road width ≥ 3 car widths** on straights (≈ 2 lanes × 1.6 cars [McMillan]), wider in corners. No corner tighter than 90° per piece [Magnopus].
7. **≥ 1 landmark per ~15 s of lap** (Frankfurt skyline, Römer, Acropolis), used selectively [Magnopus]. Destructible props along the racing line feed boost [Gamepressure, racinggames.gg].
8. **Escalation**: the easy opening third, then harder corners and the biggest jump in the final third [Magnopus].
9. **Respawn only on a fall below the track or when > 25 m off the route for > 3 s** (P). No hazard that takes control away, like the spider web [Gamepressure].
10. **No rubber-band catch-up as the main tension source** (criticised by Gamepressure). Use the routes instead.

### Proposed phone-friendly piece set (P)
- Grid cell **8 m × 8 m**: one cell = road width for 2 lanes plus shoulders, for our ~2 m-wide cars (≈ 4 car widths).
- Height step **1 m** (a ramp piece rises 2 m over 1 cell = 14°, or 4 m over 2 cells). Height range 0–12 m (13 levels).
- Rotation **90° only**. Snap by matching edge sockets (each piece edge has a type: road/offroad/water plus a height level). Tap-to-place on the grid, drag to paint straights.
- Budget **≤ 60 pieces** per track (HWU 2 allows ~30, with complex pieces costing more [progameguides]; we have simpler pieces). Each piece costs 1–3 points of a 60-point budget.
- Mode: circuit or point-to-point, like HWU [progameguides]. The start/finish piece is mandatory and placed first.

| Piece | Cells | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Start/Finish | 1×1 | Mandatory, placed first; doubles as checkpoint 0 |
| Straight | 1×1 | Road, off-road (dirt) or water variants |
| Curve 90° small | 1×1 | Radius 4 m (centre line), tight, drift-friendly |
| Curve 90° wide | 2×2 | Radius 12 m, fast and flowing |
| Banked curve 90° | 2×2 | 15° bank, keeps speed |
| S-bend | 1×2 | One lane offset |
| Ramp up/down | 1×1 or 1×2 | +2 m per cell |
| Jump kicker | 1×1 | 20° lip; pair with a gap of 1–3 empty cells and a landing ramp |
| Landing ramp | 1×2 | Downslope that keeps landings smooth |
| Bridge / raised straight | 1×1 | Auto-adds LEGO pillar supports to the ground |
| Split / merge | 2×2 | Y piece; makes alternative routes |
| Boost pad | overlay | On any road cell |
| Checkpoint gate | overlay | Auto-placed every ~6 cells; manual override allowed |
| Shore ramp (land↔water) | 1×1 | Triggers the boat swap |
| Water channel | 1×1 | Boat surface; can sit at level 0 only |
| Off-road patch | overlay | Dirt surface triggers the off-roader |
| Wall ride / half-pipe | 2×1 | Optional later; HWU-style stunt piece, NON-2K |
| Loop | 2×1 | Optional later, NON-2K (HWU); risky on phone steering |
| Prop pack | overlay | Destructible LEGO props (fill boost), Frankfurt/Athens landmarks |

- Terrain: fixed flat base map plus height via pieces (no free sculpting) (P; simpler on a phone and keeps collision matching the visuals).
- The validator rejects a track that has no closed loop, a jump with no landing, a height step > 2 m between adjacent cells, or a water piece above level 0.
