## Single most effective fix per row
1. Owner-art: the play-field text has to go.
   - Gate `LEAK.rdyMk` NEXT WAVE in `hud/explain.js` (the rdyMk wrap, line 56) behind `RET.purelook`.
   - Gate the `G.note` "New: ..." in `difficulty/endramp.js` (the sig note, line 36) behind `RET.purelook`.
   - Move the grade pop (`profile/ret.js`, RET.grade block, line 258) into the HUD band.
2. Grayscale: in `render/readable.js` (the backdrop overlay, step 1), darken the phone backdrop by about 25% in value and desaturate
   it. The art stays as made underneath, and the actors gain a value step over it.
3. Silhouette: in `render/draw.js:drawBoss` / `drawBossBody`, stop rotating the landscape boss on portrait. Until a portrait boss
   sprite exists, draw it upright and fully on screen. If that cannot work, ask Google Flow for a top-down boss (sprite list, not a tint).
4. Find-the-player: in `render/draw.js:drawPlayer`, add a brighter engine plume, a 2 px warm rim, and a ship scale of at least 1/12
   of the short side (rule 17d). On phone the visible width is about 20 px of 390, which is far below 1/12.
5. Bullets: reserve magenta for enemy shots. Recolour the player muzzle flash, engine and explosion pinks to white/cyan in
   `render/draw.js:drawPlayer`, and the explosions in the same file.
6. Clutter: same as row 1. Also move the PC weapon card (LANCE BURST / ECHO) out of the play field into the HUD frame. It is in the
   `hud/c.js` minimal-HUD block.
7. Benchmark: fix problem A below first. Everything else is judged against a scene that does not move.
- STICKER: make the shadow from 3999148 visible (it reads 0 in all 18 shots), and have the ship take the backdrop's light colour on
  its rim. The owning function is `render/draw.js:drawPlayer`. On PC the real fix is art: a pixel-art ship, or a painted city, so
  there is one style (an art request, not code).
- ENEMY VISIBILITY: in `render/draw.js`, scale up the phone drones and give them the same rim/edge treatment as the bullets
  (`render/cleanlook.js` `LGB.HK` edge), so they separate from the tower windows.

## The 3 worst problems
A. **The phone backdrop never moves.** In `render/cleanlook.js`, the `RET.clean` override of `ART.paintP(bg, scroll)` ignores
   `scroll`: it draws one static `ART.bgOf(...)` canvas. The `drawBGP` wrap also deletes the scrolling `ly-phone-*` rooftop layer for
   the call. Diffing the backdrop region between t = 14 s and t = 98 s, only 4-5% of pixels change, and all of those are actors. On
   PC, 53-60% change. The phone player flies over a fixed street-level poster, the opposite of "one picture". Fix: in that
   `ART.paintP` override, scroll the painting (a slow vertical pan or a mirrored tile, like the layer it removed), or put the
   rooftop layer back.
B. **Two styles and a rotated landscape cast on the phone.** The phone uses a ground-level painterly street (looking up at towers)
   as a top-down backdrop. Under it, the landscape sprites are turned 90°, and the boss is a sideways tank, half off the left edge.
   Nothing shares a camera. Fix: `render/draw.js:drawBoss` / `drawBossBody` portrait placement now; a top-down boss and backdrop
   from the owner's art pipeline later.
C. **The player ship is a pasted sticker that is hard to find.** On phone it is small, cool-grey and shadowless. On PC it is a
   painted HD sprite over a pixel-art city. The engine light is weak, and the magenta of the enemy shots is shared with the player's
   flame and with explosions. Fix: `render/draw.js:drawPlayer` (scale, plume, rim, visible shadow), and reserve magenta for enemies.

## One picture vs layers: 2/10
On phone, three cameras sit on top of each other: a static street-level painting, landscape sprites turned 90°, and code text and
rings. Nothing moves together, and nothing takes the scene's light. On PC the skyline does scroll and the parallax works, which is
the one thing that reads as a scene. But the pixel-art city against painted ships, and the text cards in the field, still read as
layers. Against R1/R2, where the backdrop is quieter than every actor and one light hits everything, this is a 2.
