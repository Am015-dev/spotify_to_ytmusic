## 1. Goal and one round, in my own words
You sail one junk (a boat) on a 6x6 sea. The last junk still afloat wins. On your turn you choose one of 3 current tiles, can rotate it, and lay it on the square in front of your boat. The boat then follows the gold line on that tile until the line ends. You sink if the line takes you off the edge of the board, into a leviathan (sea monster), or if a monster is sitting on the square in front of you when your turn starts. Each turn two dice are rolled. On a total of 6, 7 or 8 the leviathans "stir": they move, turn, swim off the board, smash tiles, or a new one appears. The tile badges tell you how risky each choice is: check = safe, ! = a monster could reach you on the next roll, X = sinks now.

## 2. Why I won, and why the other ship sank
- Game 1 (guided): I placed 2 tiles and nothing went wrong. Cobalt sank because a leviathan (Saltshade) was sitting on the square in front of it when its turn began. I won by doing nothing special.
- Game 2: Cobalt laid a tile that ended its route on a monster's square (Stormcoil), so it ran into a leviathan. I just placed safe-looking tiles.
- Game 3: Cobalt and I seemed to share one square at the bottom. On MY turn, Cobalt's "wake ran off the edge of the chart". As far as I can tell, my tile carried it off the board, but the game never says I caused it. It only says "It happened on your turn."
In all three games I felt like a spectator to the computer sinking itself. I had one real decision (game 3, turn 4: corner, one safe tile).

## 6. Fun verdict
**3 / 5.** It looks gorgeous: the board art, the serpents and the pink "smashes the tile" arrow are great, and the sink explanations are clear. But I won 3 out of 3 by tapping Place on whatever tile was highlighted, and the computer always sank itself. With a single easy opponent there's no tension and I never felt clever. Would I press Play again? Maybe once, against 2+ computers, to see whether it gets harder. As it is, I'd stop after about 3 games.
